## The box: "What AVI is asking for"

Four asks, each already present in a real AVI submission on `/submissions/`:

### 1. Regulate, don't prohibit

**Ask:** Replace PECA's outright ban on e-cigarettes with a regulated
market — age limits, product standards, advertising restrictions — the
same model AVI's own 2020 letter to every MP and the "Where regulation
has coincided with accelerated declines" section of `/policy/` both lay
out, and the approach nearly 100 FCTC signatory nations have taken instead
of a ban.

**Quotable line:** "Almost 100 nations chose to regulate rather than ban,
keeping vapes out of minors' hands while preserving smokers' access to a
lower-risk alternative."

Source: `src/content/submissions/2020-09-16-letter-to-mps-one-year-of-ban.md`
(points 3–4), `/policy/#regulation-works`.

### 2. A parliamentary review of the ENDS ban

**Ask:** AVI has formally asked Parliament and the government to institute
an unbiased review of the ban, rather than let it stand unexamined — the
ban was promulgated by executive ordinance, ahead of Parliament, and has
never been reviewed since.

**Quotable line:** "AVI asked Parliament and the government to reconsider
the ban and institute an unbiased review, drawing on regulatory
frameworks already in use elsewhere."

Source: `src/content/submissions/2020-09-16-letter-to-mps-one-year-of-ban.md`
(closing paragraph).

### 3. Earmark the Health Security Cess for cessation

**Ask:** Statutorily earmark 5–10% of the new Health Security Cess revenue
(introduced under the Central Excise Act, 2025) for cessation
infrastructure, harm reduction access and cancer-care subsidies — rather
than letting it vanish into general revenue while the National Tobacco
Control Programme receives a fraction of that amount annually.

**Quotable line:** "While the new Cess is levied in the name of 'Public
Health', there is no statutory provision earmarking funds to help the
crores of tobacco users who contribute this revenue."

Source: `src/content/submissions/2026-01-17-union-budget-health-security-cess.md`.

### 4. Consumer inclusion at FCTC meetings

**Ask:** Give consumer voices — people who actually use tobacco and
nicotine — real standing in India's WHO FCTC delegation and in global
tobacco-control policymaking generally, instead of policy being set
without consumer input.

**Quotable line:** "Nothing about us without us" — AVI's director,
writing in INNCO's LMIC position paper, on behalf of consumers in
low- and middle-income countries.

Source: `src/content/submissions/2020-03-13-india-position-at-fctc-cop9.md`
("without consumer input"), `/policy/#international-backing` (the INNCO
quote, already live).

**Page 1 — The case**
- Headline: "Regulation, not prohibition: the case for India's tobacco
  harm reduction policy"
- One paragraph: India's scale (roughly 100 million smokers, roughly 200
  million smokeless-tobacco users), the status-quo stat (tobacco use has
  risen, not fallen, over a decade of prohibition-only policy — the
  68.6%/45.6% household figures already on `/policy/`), and the countries
  where regulation has coincided with faster declines (Sweden, Japan, UK,
  New Zealand, Canada).

  Mexico, Australia) — illicit markets, no consumer protection, in
  Australia's case violence.
