## 1. E-cigarettes and PECA

**Position:** PECA, 2019 bans the business of e-cigarettes — sale,
manufacture, import and advertisement — not the people who use them.
Treating "banned" as if it covers personal use or possession is a
misreading the Health Ministry's own earlier public statements contradict.

**Quotable line:** "PECA bans the business of e-cigarettes, not the
people who use them — and restricting a lower-risk product only pushes
smokers back toward cigarettes."

Source: `/india/law/` (the whole page), `/about/`.

## 2. Nicotine pouches, FSSAI and CDSCO

**Position:** No law names or bans nicotine pouches. They're shut out by
a two-agency regulatory freeze instead — FSSAI's bar on nicotine as a food
ingredient, and the CDSCO/DTAB's 27 August 2026 recommendation against
approving new nicotine formulations as drugs. That's an administrative
freeze, not an Act of Parliament, and rests on the same kind of overreach
courts rejected for e-cigarettes in 2019.

**Quotable line:** "Nicotine pouches aren't banned by any law — they're
being shut out by a regulatory catch-22 between food rules and drug
rules, while a far more dangerous product stays legal on every street
corner."

Source: `/india/law/#nicotine-pouches-and-snus`.

## 3. The flight ban

**Position:** The Bureau of Civil Aviation Security's ban on carrying
e-cigarettes on flights has no basis in PECA, which doesn't touch personal
possession. AVI identified and briefed the two consumer petitioners in the
ongoing Delhi High Court case; judgment was reserved 15 September 2026.

**Quotable line:** "Carrying your own e-cigarette on a flight isn't a
crime under any Indian law — the flight ban rests on an interpretation
the government's own earlier statements contradict."

Source: `/litigation/delhi-bcas-flight-ban/`.

## 4. Police harassment

**Position:** PECA creates no offence for personal use or possession, yet
police still confiscate devices and demand cash "fines" by citing a ban
that doesn't exist for individuals. AVI tracks this and has documented the
pattern since a 2020-23 airport confiscation-report drive.

**Quotable line:** "There is no law against carrying a vape in India —
when an officer demands money to let you keep it, that's not enforcement,
it's extortion."

Source: `/india/law/#know-your-rights`, `/report-harassment/`.

## 5. Smokeless tobacco

**Position:** India has roughly 200 million smokeless-tobacco users —
nearly twice the number who smoke — yet harm reduction policy almost
never addresses them. AVI's focus has expanded from vaping to smokeless
nicotine for exactly this reason.

**Quotable line:** "India has twice as many smokeless-tobacco users as
smokers, but the policy conversation almost never includes them — harm
reduction has to mean all nicotine users, not one product category."

Source: `/about/` (opening paragraph).

## 6. WHO FCTC meetings

**Position:** AVI's director represented Indian and Global South consumers
at events on the sidelines of the WHO's Framework Convention on Tobacco
Control (and at the Global Forum on Nicotine) during his 2020-22 term as
INNCO president. AVI's position is that Global South consumer voices need
real standing in that process, not just a sideline presence.

**Quotable line:** "Global tobacco policy keeps being set without the
people most affected by it in the room — Global South consumers need a
seat at the table, not just the sidelines."

Source: `/about/` (leadership bio, INNCO paragraph).
