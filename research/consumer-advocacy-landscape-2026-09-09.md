## 1. The global consumer advocacy landscape

**The best single source found: Jerzyński et al., "Global survey of consumer
organizations advocating for safer nicotine products," *Public Health
Challenges*, 2023.**
[PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12039648/) ·
[Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/puh2.58)

- **52 active organizations** identified globally: Europe 24, Latin America
  13, Africa 8, Asia-Pacific 5, North America 2. Operating in 27 languages
  including Hindi — confirming AVI sits within a real, mapped global
  movement, not an isolated effort.
- **Funding is genuinely scarce, and that's worth knowing before assuming
  AVI is unusually under-resourced**: 31 of 52 orgs report **zero**
  funding. Of the 21 that do have funding, budgets ranged $250–$173,500
  (mean $14,753). Total funding across all 52 orgs worldwide: **$309,810**.
  None reported tobacco or pharma industry funding.
- **Common activities**: social media advocacy (85%), contacting
  legislators (85%), informing supporters (96%), press contacts (62%).
  Over three-quarters run a website + social presence.
- **Common obstacles**: volunteer-run, negative media framing, opposition
  from Bloomberg-funded anti-THR campaigns, and anticipated regulatory
  threats — taxation (30 orgs expect it), access restriction (20), flavour
  bans (34). One respondent's quote is worth keeping in mind for AVI's own
  framing: *"we have regular jobs, so the main obstacle is finding time."*

**Four regional umbrella bodies** these 52 groups cluster under — useful
for framing AVI's own position in the movement, and each is a live link
target if the site ever wants a "who we work with" mention:
- **CASA** (Campaign for Safer Alternatives) — Africa. Members include
  Vaping Saved My Life, THR Uganda, THR Nigeria, FCFA.
  [safer-alternatives.org](https://safer-alternatives.org/) (site was
  unreachable when checked — may be down or DNS-troubled; verify before
  linking)
- **ARDT Iberoamérica** — Latin America, ~10 dozen Asovape-branded groups
  across the region, led by Jeffery Zamora. Website/academy funded by
  Knowledge-Action-Change (originally Foundation for a Smoke-Free World
  money) — [INNCO profile](https://innco.org/ardt-iberoamerica/)
- **ETHRA** (European Tobacco Harm Reduction Advocates) — 24 grassroots
  orgs across 17 European countries, "voice of 27 million EU consumers."
  [ethra.co](https://ethra.co/)
- **CAPHRA** (Coalition of Asia Pacific THR Advocates) — unfunded,
  grassroots, campaigns against WHO FCTC's anti-harm-reduction stance.
  [caphraorg.net](https://caphraorg.net/) — already referenced in AVI's
  own press collection (2020 CAPHRA/FCTC COP9 letter entry)

**INNCO** (International Network of Nicotine Consumer Organisations) sits
above all four as the global federation — Samrat Chowdhery is its
president, already reflected on the site.

## 3. India tobacco use landscape

**Primary source: GATS-2 (Global Adult Tobacco Survey, India, 2016-17)** —
the last full round; a GATS-3 may exist by now, worth checking before
publishing exact figures.

- **Smoking**: 9.3–10.4% of adults currently smoke (~100 million people),
  down from 19% in 2000. Heavily gendered: ~16.7% of men vs ~1.4% of
  women.
- **Smokeless tobacco (SLT)**: **21.38%** prevalence — more than double
  smoking prevalence. This is the number that should anchor AVI's "200
  million smokeless users" framing (21.38% of India's ~1.4bn population,
  adult-only, lands in that range).
  - Breakdown: smoking-only 7.23%, SLT-only 17.94%, dual-use 3.44%.
  - Top products: **khaini, gutkha, betel quid with tobacco, zarda** — the
    actual product landscape, useful for any India-specific content that
    currently just says "smokeless tobacco" generically.
  - **Tribal communities: 32% SLT prevalence** — nearly 50% higher than
    the national rate. A genuine equity angle AVI hasn't used yet.
  - SLT use among women fell from 18.4% (GATS-1) to 12.8% (GATS-2) —
    a real decline worth knowing if anyone asks "is this getting better on
    its own."
  - Quit-attempt rates: 33.7% (smokers) vs 28.9% (SLT users) — SLT users
    attempt to quit *less* often, arguably strengthening the case that
    this population is currently underserved by harm reduction options.
- **Mortality**: ~1 million tobacco-attributable deaths/year in India
  (smoking alone; total tobacco mortality is higher when SLT is included).

**Regulatory landscape, more precisely than what's currently on
`/india/law`:**
- E-cigarettes: PECA, 2019 — total ban (manufacture, import, export,
  sale, advertisement, storage, transport, distribution). 0% legal
  prevalence.
- Heated tobacco products: also banned by law (import, sale, ad, public
  use) — not previously distinguished from e-cigarettes on the site;
  worth a one-line mention since it's a distinct product category readers
  may ask about.
- Snus: banned, sale and advertising.
- **Nicotine pouches — an update to the site's current framing.** AVI's
  `/india/law` currently frames this as "FSSAI hasn't approved nicotine as
  a food additive, so no lawful pathway exists" — that's the food-safety
  mechanism. But a **September 2025** development adds a second,
  more direct blocking mechanism: India's **National Tobacco Control
  Programme (NTCP) has recommended rejecting manufacturing/import license
  applications for oral nicotine pouches** outright.
  [TobaccoIntelligence, Sept 2025](https://tobaccointelligence.com/india-oral-tobacco-and-nicotine-pouch-regulation-september-2025/)
  — **I could not find the primary government notification for this** (the
  GSTHR country page cites it but doesn't name the specific instrument),
  so treat this as a lead to verify with a primary source before
  publishing it as fact, not as confirmed as the FSSAI mechanism already
  on the site.
- NRT (gums, patches): legal, government-supported, regulated as
  medicine under the Drugs & Cosmetics Act — this contrast (illegal
  reduced-risk nicotine vs legal-and-subsidized NRT of similar or lower
  real-world quit efficacy per Cochrane, see §4) is one of AVI's strongest
  rhetorical points and is already implicit in the new homepage copy.

**India-specific primary research** (not yet cited anywhere on the site —
genuine content gap, and directly answers "does this even work for
Indians specifically," which is exactly what a skeptical policymaker or
doctor would ask):

> **Patterns of tobacco and e-cigarette use status in India: a
> cross-sectional survey of 3000 vapers in eight Indian cities.**
> *Harm Reduction Journal*, 2020. [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7106589/) ·
> [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12954-020-00362-7)
>
> 3,000 vapers surveyed across India's 8 largest metros (81.4% male,
> median age 29). **71.3% of smokers reported quitting (30.0%) or
> reducing (41.3%) smoking with e-cigarettes**; similar patterns held for
> SLT users. 79% believed e-cigarettes less harmful than smoking; 80%
> first used nicotine via smoking or SLT (i.e., not vaping-as-gateway).

I added this as a draft `/science` entry — see §5.

## 4. Evidence base to draw on

**Cochrane review (living systematic review, most current update)**
[Cochrane Library](https://www.cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD010216.pub10/full) ·
[plain-language summary](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD010216_can-electronic-cigarettes-help-people-stop-smoking-and-do-they-have-any-unwanted-effects-when-used)

The single best "does it actually work" citation, and more current than
the RCP 2016 report already on the site: **per 100 people trying to quit,
8–10 succeed with nicotine e-cigarettes, vs 6 with NRT, vs 4 with no
support.** This is a *head-to-head beats NRT* finding — stronger and more
citable than a general "e-cigarettes are less harmful" claim, and directly
undercuts the "just use the gums and patches" line policymakers reach for
first. I added this as a draft `/science` entry — see §5.

**Sweden/snus — the best available real-world natural experiment**, and
unusually relevant to AVI specifically because snus is smokeless, making
it a closer analogy to India's SLT population than to vaping:
[PMC: "Snus: a compelling harm reduction alternative to cigarettes"](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6882181/)
- Sweden: ~5% daily smoking (lowest in the EU) against ~20% daily "oral
  tobacco" (snus) use.
- 61% lower lung cancer incidence in Swedish men vs the EU average; 44%
  lower tobacco-attributable mortality overall.
- Modelled EU-wide impact if other countries matched Sweden's pattern:
  54% reduction in male lung cancer mortality.
- **Caveat for India content**: Swedish snus is a manufactured,
  quality-controlled product very different from khaini/gutkha/zarda —
  don't imply Indian SLT products carry Swedish-snus-level risk reduction
  as-is. The honest version of this argument is "reduced-risk smokeless
  products exist and work at population scale," not "Indian smokeless
  tobacco is already safe."

**Clive Bates / The Counterfactual** — [clivebates.com](https://clivebates.com/),
also on [Substack](https://clivebates.substack.com/). His
[Q&A resource](https://clivebates.com/vaping-tobacco-harm-reduction-nicotine-science-and-policy-q-a/)
is a ready-made ~60-question framework across 10 topics (strategy,
relative risk, quitting efficacy, youth/gateway, regulation, public-place
use, marketing, retail, industry involvement, myth-rebuttals) — this is
close to a template for an eventual AVI FAQ or expanded `/science`
structure, if the site ever wants one. No India-specific Bates piece was
found in this pass; worth a direct site search on clivebates.com later
rather than concluding none exists.

**Safer Nicotine Wiki** — the resource the site owner meant by "nicotine
wiki": [safernicotine.wiki](https://safernicotine.wiki/mediawiki/index.php?amp=&title=Main_Page).
A MediaWiki-style community-maintained encyclopedia of THR evidence —
useful as a *starting index* to find primary sources, not itself a
citable primary source for the site (it's a wiki).

**Reddit**: no India-specific vaping/THR subreddit found in either search
or the academic literature. General quit-vaping communities exist
(r/QuitVaping, r/QuittingJuul, r/stopvaping, r/quit_vaping) and have been
studied academically for support-seeking behaviour, but there's no
existing India-specific online community to plug into or reference.

**Substacks**: Clive Bates' is the only one identified as a going
concern in this pass. Given the fast-moving nature of Substack, a fresh
search closer to when content is actually drafted would catch anything
newer.
