Days after the central government promulgated the Prohibition of Electronic
Cigarettes Ordinance, 2019 — bypassing Parliament and banning sale,
manufacture, import, export, distribution and advertisement of ENDS, with
penalties of up to a year in jail — AVI organised a nationwide protest at
Jantar Mantar, Delhi and in parallel venues in five other cities, all on the
same afternoon.

City leads and volunteer coordinators ran each location: Jantar Mantar in
Delhi, the Mumbai Marathi Patrakar Sangh in Mumbai, Lamakaan in Hyderabad,
Freedom Park in Bengaluru, Raj Park Hotel in Chennai, and the Press Club in
Kolkata. Organisers requested and received local police permission in
advance, and set clear ground rules for participants: no minors, no
littering, no vaping in public during the protest itself, and calm,
respectful engagement with anyone who asked questions.

The protest's core argument, carried on placards and in the accompanying
press release: 65 nations regulate e-cigarettes rather than banning them;
the ordinance was rushed through without the research or public hearings a
policy affecting an estimated 11 crore Indian smokers should have had; and
the ban would not stop use, only push it into an unregulated black market
with no age checks or quality control — exactly what happened in the years
that followed.
