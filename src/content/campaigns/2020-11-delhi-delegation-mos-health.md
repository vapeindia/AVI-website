Around the same time AVI's India-specific policy input was cited in a
global report on tobacco harm reduction, an AVI delegation secured a direct
meeting with Anupriya Patel, then Union Minister of State for Health &amp;
Family Welfare, to make the case in person: that a blanket ban denies India's
roughly 27 crore tobacco users — of whom over 10 crore smoke — access to a
lower-risk way to quit or switch, and that regulation, not prohibition, is
what the evidence from the 65-plus nations that allow e-cigarettes actually
supports.
