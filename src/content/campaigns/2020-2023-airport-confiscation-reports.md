Days after the Bureau of Civil Aviation Security began enforcing a ban on
carrying e-cigarettes on flights in January 2020, AVI opened a short public
form asking affected travellers to report what had happened: which
airport, which airline and flight, roughly when, which official
confiscated the device, and what exactly was taken. It also asked two
forward-looking questions — would you be willing to join a court case
against these confiscations, and would you contribute funds toward one.

Over the next three years the form collected **60 individual reports**
from across the country. Mumbai and Delhi accounted for the largest share
(15 and 10 reports respectively), with Bangalore, Hyderabad, Goa, Kochi,
Chennai and Pune also represented. A number of respondents were
international travellers — from the UK, the US, Germany and elsewhere —
caught out mid-transit with no warning that a personal vape would be
taken at security. Of the 60, **42 (70%) said they'd be willing to join a
legal challenge**, and 38 (63%) were willing to help fund one — a
genuine base of affected consumers, not a hypothetical one, at a point
when AVI was still deciding whether this fight was worth mounting.

That base is what it became: AVI drew on these reports to identify and
brief the two consumer petitioners now named in its
[ongoing Delhi High Court case against the flight ban](/litigation/delhi-bcas-flight-ban) —
the same underlying complaint dozens of respondents had already
documented, now argued in court as evidence the ban lacks any basis in
demonstrated in-flight risk.
