Two out of three people who smoke die from it — and AVI's argument has
always been that switching to vaping significantly reduces that risk. The
#WarOnTar campaign set out to make that case with real-world data rather
than argument alone: vapers and ex-smokers were asked to take two low-cost
lung-health tests (₹1,200–1,500) at a centre near them, which doubled as a
free health check for participants, and submit the results through a
confidential AVI survey form.

The goal was twofold — give participants a genuine read on their own lung
health, including any lingering effects of past smoking, and build a
dataset large enough to be worth putting in front of India's health
ministry as part of AVI's ongoing push against the vaping ban.
