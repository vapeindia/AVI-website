On 27 August 2026 the Drugs Technical Advisory Board (DTAB) recommended
that no new nicotine formulations be approved as drugs — closing the last
lawful route to market for nicotine pouches in India. Nicotine pouches
contain no tobacco leaf, so COTPA doesn't apply to them; that pushes them
to the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI), whose
Regulation 2.3.4 already bars nicotine as a food ingredient. With the DTAB
recommendation, the only other legal home — treating nicotine as a drug
under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act — is closed too.

That leaves nicotine pouches with no lawful pathway to market at all, by a
two-agency regulatory freeze rather than any Act of Parliament naming or
banning them. AVI has seen this pattern before: in 2019, courts rejected
the same kind of executive overreach when it was used against
e-cigarettes, because a consumer product was being forced into "drug"
classification despite no manufacturer making a therapeutic claim for it.
No nicotine pouch is sold in India as a cessation aid — they are marketed
as consumer products, the same as abroad.

The evidence on relative risk is not in dispute. The US FDA's own June
2026 modified-risk marketing order found that nicotine pouches put users
at lower risk of several smoking-related diseases than cigarettes.
Meanwhile, loose smokeless tobacco products — gutka, khaini — stay legal
and openly sold at every paan shop, causing hundreds of thousands of
deaths a year. Closing off a lower-risk alternative while a far more
dangerous one stays on the market isn't caution; it runs against the
basic logic of harm reduction.
