[DATE OF JUDGMENT] — The Delhi High Court has ruled in favour of the two
consumer petitioners, Dr Kiran Harsha Melkote (W.P.(C) 2786/2023) and
Sutirtha Dutta (W.P.(C) 5485/2022), in their challenge to the Bureau of
Civil Aviation Security's ban on carrying e-cigarettes on flights. AVI
identified and briefed both petitioners, retained counsel, and has
directed this litigation since it began.

The Court's judgment confirms what AVI has argued throughout: the flight
ban was never based on the Prohibition of Electronic Cigarettes Act, 2019
(PECA) — PECA bans the sale, manufacture and advertisement of
e-cigarettes, not personal possession — and BCAS's own circular was never
supported by evidence of in-flight risk. The ruling removes a restriction
that conflicted with the government's own stated position that
e-cigarettes should be regulated, not prohibited outright.

This case began with a crowdsourced public record: from January 2020, AVI
ran a confiscation-report form that logged 60 incidents nationwide over
three years, most heavily out of Mumbai and Delhi. That pattern — personal
devices seized with no process, no evidence of risk and no route to get
them back — is the core of what the Court has now addressed.
