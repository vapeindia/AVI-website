[DATE OF JUDGMENT] — The Delhi High Court has ruled against the two
consumer petitioners, Dr Kiran Harsha Melkote (W.P.(C) 2786/2023) and
Sutirtha Dutta (W.P.(C) 5485/2022), upholding the Bureau of Civil Aviation
Security's ban on carrying e-cigarettes on flights. AVI identified and
briefed both petitioners, retained counsel, and directed this litigation
since it began — the result is a setback, not the end of the underlying
question.

AVI's position is unchanged by this ruling: the Prohibition of Electronic
Cigarettes Act, 2019 (PECA) bans the sale, manufacture and advertisement
of e-cigarettes, not personal possession, and no provision anywhere in
PECA makes carrying a device for personal use an offence. [AVI is
reviewing the judgment with counsel to decide next steps, including a
possible appeal.]

This case began with a crowdsourced public record: from January 2020, AVI
ran a confiscation-report form that logged 60 incidents nationwide over
three years, most heavily out of Mumbai and Delhi. That pattern — personal
devices seized with no process, no evidence of risk and no route to get
them back — is the problem this litigation set out to address, and it
remains unresolved.
