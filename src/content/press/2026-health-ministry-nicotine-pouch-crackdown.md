On 19 September 2026 the Union Health Ministry ordered states to crack
down on the sale of nicotine pouches — enforcement action reported by
Reuters on 25 September 2026. No nicotine pouch is named or banned by any
Act of Parliament. The basis for this crackdown is the same regulatory
freeze AVI has already set out at
[/india/law/](/india/law/#nicotine-pouches-and-snus): nicotine pouches
contain no tobacco leaf, so they fall outside COTPA and are pushed to the
Food Safety and Standards Authority of India, whose rules already bar
nicotine as a food ingredient, while the Drugs Technical Advisory Board's
27 August 2026 recommendation closed the only other legal route, treating
new nicotine formulations as unapprovable drugs.

A state enforcement drive built on that foundation is enforcing a ban that
Parliament never passed. This is the same pattern AVI fought — and
courts rejected — when it was used against e-cigarettes: a consumer
product forced into a stricter legal category than the evidence supports,
while a demonstrably more dangerous product, loose smokeless tobacco, stays
legal and freely sold. India has roughly 200 million smokeless tobacco
users against around 100 million smokers; cutting off a lower-risk
alternative for people who already use nicotine doesn't reduce nicotine
use, it just removes the safer option.

AVI is seeking the actual text of the Health Ministry's order and the
affected states' enforcement directions before taking a litigation
position. This statement sets out AVI's reading of the underlying law;
it is not an offer to sell, supply or facilitate access to anything.
