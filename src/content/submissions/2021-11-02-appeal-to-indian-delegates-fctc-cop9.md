By the time the WHO FCTC's 9th Conference of Parties actually convened —
postponed a year by the pandemic from its original Netherlands date — AVI
enclosed its own appeal to India's delegation alongside a second
document: an open letter to COP9 delegates signed by 100 specialists in
nicotine science, policy and practice from around the world. Both were
sent to the Health Secretary and India's delegation ahead of the
conference; AVI's own covering letter is reproduced below in full, the
100-specialist letter is not separately reproduced here.

**Full text of AVI's letter:**

Honorable Sir/Madam,

I am writing to you on behalf of Association for Vapers India (AVI), a
consumer-led nonprofit that advocates for the rights of India's 270
million tobacco users to avail less harmful substitutes and for the
adoption of harm reduction measures in tobacco control.

AVI has issued an appeal to the Indian delegates to the upcoming 9th
Conference of Parties (COP9) of WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco
Control (FCTC) requesting for transparency and inclusion of consumers in
policymaking, making risk-reduction a part of tobacco control efforts,
and allowing LMICs to develop solutions suited to their needs.

We have included in our appeal another letter to COP9 delegates signed
by 100 specialists in nicotine science, policy and practice, urging them
to take a more positive stance on tobacco harm reduction and help
millions of tobacco users worldwide.

We urge you to consider both the appeals and include them in your stance
during this important global conference, as well as for formulating
public health policy in India.

For Association for Vapers India,

Pratik Gupta,
Director
