Sent to Union Health Minister Mansukh Mandaviya ahead of the WHO FCTC's
10th Conference of Parties in Panama (February 2024), CC'd to the
Secretary (Health & Family Welfare) and several MPs, this letter also
enclosed evidence briefs on vaping's relative safety, cessation
effectiveness, youth impact and regulatory approach, authored by Clive
Bates, former director of Action on Smoking and Health (ASH UK).

**Full text of the email:**

To

Union minister of Health and Family welfare,

New Delhi.

Respected Shri (Dr) Mansukh Mandaviyaji,

Association of Vapers India or AVI is a charity seeking access to risk
reduction for India's 27 crore tobacco users. We believe traditional
approaches to tobacco control, mostly punitive in nature such as tax hikes
and use restrictions, should be complemented with humane harm reduction
measures: offering adequate cessation support and respecting people's
agency by allowing tobacco users to move down the harm spectrum by
switching to demonstrably lower-risk alternatives – vaping, snus and
nicotine pouches.

We are writing to you ahead of the Conference of Parties (COP10) of WHO's
Framework for Tobacco Control (FCTC) which will be held from February 5
onwards in Panama. India being a signatory to the FCTC will be present at
the conference through its health ministry and will play a significant
role in deciding the future course of global tobacco control.

We wish the Indian delegation the best and are confident it will do
everything necessary to champion the interests and needs of our millions
of tobacco users, most of whom are from poor sections of society, can
ill-afford medical requirements arising out of long-term tobacco use and
have weak cessation support, while protecting our youth from falling into
the trap of tobacco dependence.

As former smokers who have benefited from risk reduction, we wish to
communicate our views and concerns which we hope the Indian delegation
will take into account. While India's ban on low-risk electronic nicotine
delivery systems (ENDS), though tragic and harmful, is an internal
matter, we urge the delegation to resist WHO's hardline stance against
lower-risk alternatives and not commit to further jeopardising access to
harm reduction, which is enshrined in Article 1(d) of the FCTC treaty.

At this stage it should suffice to point out that our ENDS ban has not
only been wholly ineffective since illicit vaping devices are now being
sold even in small towns across the country, creating a huge illegal
market, but it has also failed to protect the youth for whom the ban was
intended as they now have even more, unrestricted access to these
products, an allure created through illegality which regulated products
would not have. Further, there is no safety or consumer protection, and
significant loss to revenue generation.

Instead, we request the Indian delegation to take this opportunity at
COP10 to learn from the experiences of western nations who have allowed,
and in some cases promoted (UK, NZ, Japan, Sweden etc.), switching their
tobacco-using populations to less risky alternatives. Tobacco use is
declining sharply in these nations, some of whom are on track to
eliminate smoking. We should find ways to emulate these successes as we
bear an enormous tobacco burden which adversely impacts our economic and
social progress.

To assist the delegation with the latest evidence on safer alternatives,
we are sharing evidence briefs published by renowned tobacco harm
reduction expert and former director of Action for Smoking & Health (ASH
UK), Clive Bates, which touch upon four major issues – relative safety of
vaping versus smoking, effectiveness of vaping in helping smokers quit,
its impact on youth, and importantly, how should these innovations be
regulated.

We hope you will find this communication helpful.

Respectfully yours,

Pratik Gupta

Director,

Association of Vapers India
